## 2. Summary of the Recorded Stage

According to the README, the four-neck flask contained xylene and PP, and the PP granules had dissolved completely in xylene before transfer. After the assembly was removed from the paraffin bath at the recorded temperature of **140 °C**, the flask was immediately removed from the bath and its contents were transferred through a glass funnel into another glass vessel.

During the transfer, part of the PP adhered to two glass surfaces in a jelly-like, sticky state:

1. The inner wall of the four-neck flask.
2. The tube connected to the glass funnel.

The README attributes the adhesion to temperature changes during transfer. The actual solution temperature at the time of transfer and its cooling history are not recorded in this folder. This attribution is therefore reported as the explanation written in the README, not as an independently measured cause.

## 3. Timeline of Operations and Observations

### 3.1. Removal from the Bath and Transfer of the Solution

The README records the following:

- The four-neck flask contained a xylene/PP solution.
- The PP granules had dissolved completely in xylene before transfer.
- After the assembly was removed from the paraffin bath at the recorded temperature of 140 °C, it was immediately taken out of the bath.
- The solution was transferred through a glass funnel into another glass vessel.
- During transfer, some PP adhered to the inner wall of the flask and some adhered to the tube connected to the funnel.
- The adhering material was described as jelly-like and sticky at this stage.

Note: The README specifies 140 °C as the temperature of the paraffin bath. This folder does not report an independently measured temperature of the PP/xylene solution at the moment of transfer.

### 3.2. Rinsing the Flask with Tap Water

According to the README, the four-neck flask was thoroughly rinsed with tap water for **2 minutes** in an attempt to clean it. Despite this rinsing, the adhesion of the material to the flask wall was not removed.

The README also explicitly states that the following actions were not performed:

- Scraping the flask wall to remove the material.
- Using soap or soap foam.
- Forcibly or mechanically detaching the adhered material.

These are recorded as actions that were not performed. The README does not provide a separate reason for omitting each of them.

## 5. Separation of Observations, Reports, and Conclusions

- Complete dissolution of PP granules in xylene before transfer.
- Immediate transfer of the solution from the four-neck flask through a glass funnel into another vessel.
- Adhesion of some PP to the inner flask wall and funnel tube.

## 7. Documented Summary

At this stage, the PP-containing solution, which according to the README had dissolved completely in xylene before transfer, was removed from a paraffin bath at the recorded temperature of 140 °C and immediately transferred through a glass funnel into another vessel. During transfer, some material adhered to the inner wall of the four-neck flask and some to the funnel tube. The adhering material was initially jelly-like and sticky.

A two-minute tap-water rinse of the flask did not remove the adhesion. According to the report, the material remained attached to the flask wall approximately one hour later. After one day, however, the residue could be detached easily and could be powdered. The material remaining in the funnel tube had also lost its jelly-like and sticky state after one day and could be removed easily.

The README raises the possibility that xylene remained in the residue and states that washing was necessary. However, the residual solvent content was not measured in this folder.

**Key point:** This folder documents a change in the visible and reported handling behavior of material adhering to the flask and funnel over an interval of approximately one hour to one day. These observations alone are insufficient to determine the physical or chemical cause of the change, confirm PP purity, or calculate a recovery yield.
