## 2. Summary of the Recorded Procedure

According to the README, the PP-containing solution prepared in the four-neck flask with xylene was transferred into a pre-cooled glass bottle. The recorded purpose was to cool the solution in an ice-water bath and form a gel-like precipitate.

The overall sequence, as written in the README, is:

1. The glass bottle was placed in an ice-water bath at a recorded temperature of 1 to 5 °C for one minute.
2. The four-neck flask was removed from the paraffin bath at the recorded temperature of 140 °C.
3. The solution was immediately transferred through a glass funnel into the pre-cooled bottle.
4. The bottle containing the solution was kept in the cold bath for another 15 minutes.
5. At the end of this period, a soft, transparent, accumulated gel-like precipitate had formed.
6. The bottle contents were poured into a Büchner funnel mounted on a vacuum flask for separation of the precipitate.
7. No vacuum was applied during this separation.
8. After 15 minutes, the filter paper and wet precipitate were removed from the Büchner funnel.
9. The precipitate was wrapped in several layers of filter paper for drying.

Important note: The README records 1 to 5 °C for pre-cooling the bottle. This folder does not provide a continuous temperature log for the entire 15-minute cooling period. It therefore does not establish that the bath remained exactly within this range throughout that period.

### 4.2. Why Vacuum Was Not Used

The README gives two reasons for not carrying out vacuum filtration:

- A vacuum tap/line was not available.
- The vacuum pump was not used because it was difficult to control and excessive suction was to be avoided. The stated concern was that the precipitate and filter paper might be drawn into the vacuum flask.

Therefore, although the Büchner funnel was mounted on a vacuum flask, **no vacuum suction was applied in this recorded run**.

This distinction is important: the presence of a vacuum flask and Büchner funnel does not mean that vacuum filtration was performed. The README explicitly describes separation without vacuum.

The folder does not record the time required for liquid passage, the volume collected in the flask, the amount of liquid remaining in the precipitate, or the filtration rate. The quantitative performance of this separation step therefore cannot be evaluated.

## 8. Suggestions for More Complete Quantitative Recording

The following items are not recorded in the current README and are suggestions only for improving the quantitative record in a future run:

1. Record the PP mass and total xylene volume in the solution before transferring it into the cold bottle.
2. Measure the actual solution temperature during transfer and record the cold-bath temperature at the beginning and end of the 15-minute period.
3. Record the approximate volume of solution/filtrate collected in the flask and, if possible, its mass.
4. Weigh the dry filter paper before separation and after drying, with the weighing conditions recorded.
5. Record the dry precipitate mass and calculate recovery relative to the initial PP mass.
6. Record the drying duration and conditions, including temperature, time, and air exposure.
7. Record the appearance of the precipitate after drying and whether it actually becomes powderable.

These items must not be interpreted as having been performed in the experiment described in this folder.

## 9. Documented Summary

In this stage, the receiving bottle was first pre-cooled for one minute in an ice-water bath at the recorded temperature of 1 to 5 °C. The four-neck flask was then removed from the paraffin bath at the recorded temperature of 140 °C, and the solution was immediately transferred through a glass funnel into the bottle. The bottle containing the solution was kept in the cold bath for 15 minutes. According to the README, an accumulated, gel-like, soft, transparent precipitate formed.

The solution and precipitate were then transferred to a Büchner funnel mounted on a vacuum flask. Although the Büchner/vacuum-flask arrangement was used, no vacuum was applied because a vacuum tap/line was unavailable and uncontrolled suction was a concern. After 15 minutes, the wet and soft precipitate remained on the filter paper and was wrapped in several layers of filter paper for drying.

The README expects that the precipitate may become powderable after drying and may resemble the sediment recorded in the 3_1- Sediment folder. However, this outcome has not yet been observed or confirmed in the photographs or data contained in this folder.

**Key point:** This folder documents a more deliberate cooling sequence in the receiving vessel, followed by Büchner separation without applied vacuum. The wet, transparent precipitate is visually documented, but dry mass, recovery yield, purity, and final powderability are not confirmed results in this folder.
