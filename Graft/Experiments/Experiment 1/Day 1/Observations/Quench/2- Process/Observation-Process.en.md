# Technical Record of Observations: Quench / 2- Process

## 1. Scope and Source Materials

This document covers only the contents of **Quench/2- Process** for Experiment 1, Day 1. The materials reviewed in this folder are:

- **README.docx** and the observations written in it.
- Five video files, **فرایند 0.mp4** through **فرایند 4.mp4**.
- One photograph, **فرایند 5.jpg**.

The visual descriptions are based on reviewable frames from all five videos and the still photograph. Elapsed experimental times such as 10 or 30 minutes are quoted from the README; they are not the durations of the video files. The actual clips are only about 5.8 to 19.3 seconds long.

This document does not enter the sediment-collection folders or other experimental stages. No results from those stages have been imported into this record.

## 2. Summary of the Recorded Process

According to the README, the quench process described in this folder is intended to last **60 minutes** in total. The initial charge and stirring conditions are recorded as follows:

| Parameter | Recorded value |
|---|---:|
| Polypropylene (PP) mass | 2 g |
| Initial xylene volume | 10 mL |
| Initial xylene-to-PP ratio | 5 mL per g |
| Magnetic stirring speed | 500 rpm |
| Total process duration, according to README | 60 min |
| Additional xylene added during the final 20 min | 10 mL |
| Total xylene after the addition | 20 mL |
| Final xylene-to-PP ratio | 10 mL per g |

The ratios calculated from the reported quantities are:

- At the start: 10 mL of xylene for 2 g of PP, equivalent to 5 mL/g.
- After adding another 10 mL: 20 mL of total xylene for 2 g of PP, equivalent to 10 mL/g.

The README attributes the initial ratio of 1 g PP to 5 mL xylene to published articles. No bibliographic details or reference numbers are specified in this folder. Accordingly, this document records the ratio as stated in the README but does not claim to have independently matched it to a particular article.

## 3. Timeline of Recorded Observations and Process Changes

### 3.1. Approximately 10 Minutes After Start: Process 0 Video

According to the README, approximately 10 minutes had elapsed from the start of the experiment when Process 0 was recorded.

The README records the following observations:

- The PP granules looked like opaque, glass-like granules.
- The granules were moving in xylene under magnetic stirring at 500 rpm.
- The granules had not dissolved completely, but had become softer and more transparent.
- The diameter of some granules had decreased.
- The README author interpreted these changes as signs that dissolution had begun, not as complete dissolution.

The video shows the flask and bath assembly from several angles, but part of the footage is directed toward hoses and surrounding apparatus components. Because of the camera angle, glass reflections, and the short duration of the clip, the granule size or count cannot be determined quantitatively from the video.

### 3.2. Approximately 30 Minutes After Start: Process 1 Video

The README places this video at approximately 30 minutes after the experiment began.

The main observation recorded in the README is that, during the first nine seconds of the video, bubbles rise from the lower part of the flask toward the liquid surface.

The origin of the bubbles is explicitly left unresolved in the README. Xylene boiling and a possible relation to granule dissolution are both raised as possibilities, but neither is identified as the confirmed cause.

Bubble-like regions and surface disturbance can be seen in the reviewable frames. However, the image quality and camera angle are insufficient to determine the exact point of bubble formation, their path, or the identity of the gas. The bubbles therefore do not, by themselves, prove that xylene was boiling or that a specific reaction was occurring.

### 3.3. Floating Granules: Process 2 Video

The README does not specify the exact elapsed experimental time for this clip.

The written record states that:

- Some granules floated on the liquid surface.
- These floating granules did not move with the rotating magnetic stir bar.
- Other granules continued to move with the stir bar.

The video provides a closer view of the vessel and stirring region. Bright objects or regions are visible at the surface and around the liquid. ### 3.4. Final 20 Minutes: Process 3 Video and Xylene Addition

According to the README, this video relates to the final 20 minutes of the process.

The sequence of events reported is:

1. As the process continued, more PP granules dissolved.
2. More gel formed at the liquid surface and accumulated.
3. The gel accumulation reportedly began to affect the movement of the magnetic stir bar.
4. The stir bar occasionally moved away from the center and approached the flask wall.
5. To improve the situation, **an additional 10 mL of xylene** was added during the final 20 minutes.
6. The process reportedly proceeded better after this addition.
7. According to the README, all PP had dissolved by the end of the 60-minute period, producing a uniform solution with no visible surface gel.

Taking into account the initial 10 mL of xylene and the additional 10 mL, the total xylene volume became 20 mL. The final recorded ratio for this run was therefore 10 mL of xylene per gram of PP.

The video shows portions of the apparatus and the bath/vessel region, but the available frames do not independently or quantitatively verify the amount of gel, the degree of stir-bar deflection, or complete PP dissolution. These points remain operational observations reported in the README.

## 4. Condenser Review: Process 4 Video and Process 5 Photograph

The README states that Process 4 video and Process 5 photograph show the condenser around the middle of the experiment. The written note refers to bubbles around and outside the spiral tubes, as well as inside the spiral path. It also notes that bubbles inside the spiral are difficult to see because xylene is colorless.

### 4.1. What Is Visible in the Media

- The glass spiral section of the condenser is visible in both media files.
- Several bright, bubble-like regions appear along the spiral.
- Some scattered bubbles or bubble-like regions are also visible in the space around the spiral tube.
- The Process 5 photograph provides a closer still view of the spiral and the bubble-like regions.
- Image quality, reflections from the glass, and camera movement make it difficult to identify the precise location of the bubbles.

### 4.2. Is the Condenser Operating Properly?

**The video and photograph alone are not sufficient to confirm either correct condenser operation or a condenser fault.**

The media do not establish exactly which fluid path contains the bubble-like regions. Without positively identifying the cooling-water path and the vapor/condensate path, it is not possible to determine whether each bubble is trapped air in the cooling circuit, gas in another passage, or an optical reflection/distortion.

The following operating details also cannot be reliably established from these media:

- The actual cooling-water inlet and outlet orientation.
- Continuity and flow rate of the cooling-water circuit.
- Cooling-water inlet and outlet temperatures.
- Whether all connections are leak-free.
- Whether condensate returns continuously from the condenser to the flask.
- Whether xylene vapor escapes from the upper end of the system.

Therefore, the presence of bubbles is not by itself proof of a fault. Likewise, seeing an apparently intact glass coil does not establish adequate thermal performance.

### 4.3. Information Needed for a More Conclusive Assessment

The actual cooling-water route and vapor path for this specific condenser must first be identified. The following items can then be checked and recorded separately:

1. Identify the cooling-water inlet and outlet from the actual equipment connections, rather than guessing from the photograph.
2. Check for continuous water flow and confirm that the cooling passage is filled and purged of trapped air, if the bubbles are located in that circuit.
3. Measure or record the cooling-water inlet/outlet temperatures and flow rate.
4. Observe the lower condenser-to-flask connection and check for condensate return while the system is operating.
5. Check the connections for leaks and inspect the upper part of the system for possible vapor escape.

These are proposed checks for a more complete assessment. They are not claims that the checks were performed during the recorded experiment.

## 5. Separation of Recorded Data from Hypotheses

The README raises several possible explanations for the surface gel formation and the need to add xylene:

- Excessive xylene evaporation.
- Slow condensation or reflux, with less xylene returning from the condenser to the flask.
- An insufficient initial ratio of 1 g PP to 5 mL xylene for the conditions of this experiment.

These are presented as possibilities in the README. No independent measurement in this folder proves any of them.

Adding 10 mL of xylene and observing an improvement afterward is an important practical observation. However, this single change does not establish whether the original problem was caused by insufficient solvent, xylene evaporation, condenser performance, or a combination of factors. It is also a single run without a controlled comparison, so it cannot by itself establish that a 1:5 ratio is generally inadequate.

## 7. Documented Summary

In this run, 2 g of PP was initially processed in 10 mL of xylene at a stirring speed of 500 rpm. The recorded observations include granule softening and increased transparency, bubbles of unresolved origin, floating granules, and accumulation of surface gel. During the final 20 minutes, another 10 mL of xylene was added. The README reports improved processing and complete PP dissolution by the end of the 60-minute period.

Regarding the condenser, the video and photograph show bubble-like regions around and along the spiral. They are not sufficient to judge condenser integrity, cooling-water flow, or condensation efficiency. That assessment requires simultaneous observation of the connections, cooling-water circulation, and condensate return.

**Key point:** The initial ratio of 5 mL xylene per gram of PP and the final ratio of 10 mL/g must be recorded separately. The reason for the additional solvent and the origin of the condenser bubbles have not been established by the data in this folder.
